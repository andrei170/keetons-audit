Pre-Call Audit · Keetons Landscaping and Fencing

Your Biggest Job Was £25,000. Your Website Is Selling Garden Maintenance.

Prepared for Thornley Keeton · 5 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
What You Told Our Team
The brief, as it reached us
  • Your own fencing and landscaping company, in business since October.
  • Booked out for the last six months on referrals and word of mouth.
  • One to three clients a week, varying, because some jobs take far longer than others.
  • ⭐ Biggest recent job: £25,000.
  • Never advertised, because you have been too busy.
  • Books are now running thin and you want work booked in as soon as possible.

⭐ Of the businesses we have looked at this week, you are the only one whose problem genuinely is that you need more work. The others told us they could not resource what they already had. So this is a straightforward conversation and we think we can help.

⛔ But we are not going to start advertising on Monday, and the two sections below explain why that would waste your money rather than earn it.

🔴 Finding One: Your Website Would Buy You The Wrong Customers
🔴🔴 There are two businesses described on your site, and the money is only in one of them

Here is what your own homepage says, word for word:

  • “your trusted partner for comprehensive garden maintenance and outdoor solutions”
  • “Transforming your space, regardless of size or budget”
  • “We believe everyone deserves to enjoy nature and the outdoors”
  • “no matter the size of your garden or budget”
  • “especially for those who find the physical aspect of gardening challenging”
  • Your first service heading: “Garden transformations — Comprehensive garden maintenance services to keep your green space thriving”

⚠️ And your email address is keetonsgardens@gmail.com.

🔴 Read as a customer would read it, that is a garden maintenance business aimed at people who want help with jobs they can no longer manage, at any budget. It is warm, it is decent, and it is nothing like a company that builds £25,000 landscaping schemes.

🔴🔴 Now consider what happens if we advertise that site. Advertising is extremely good at producing more of whatever you are already asking for. So we would spend your money filling your diary with fence repairs, bits of turf and tidy-ups — low value, high admin, and exactly the work that occupies a week without paying for one.

⭐ Your books would fill. That is the danger, not the reassurance. You would be busy, poorer, and convinced advertising does not work. The positioning page deals with this properly, because it is the single most important thing we have to say to you.

⚠️ Finding Two: Your Books May Be Thin Because It Is October
⚠️ Worth establishing before you spend a penny

You were booked out for six months and are now thin. ⭐ Those six months were roughly April to September. The thin period starts in October.

⚠️ That is the shape of almost every landscaping business in the north east, and it may mean you do not have a demand problem at all — you have a seasonal one. Homeowners commission gardens in spring, they stop thinking about them when the clocks go back, and they start again in February.

🔴 This matters enormously for what you should do with your money.

  • If it is seasonal: advertising hard in November and December buys the least interested audience of the year at full price. ⛔ The wrong month is an expensive way to learn this. The right move is to spend the quiet period getting the foundations right and be ready and visible before the spring enquiries start, not after.
  • If it is not seasonal — if your referrals have genuinely dried up for other reasons — then it is urgent and we should act now.

⚠️ You have only been trading since October last year, so you have had one season. That is not enough history to know which of the two it is, and neither of us should assume. ⭐ But there is work that is far less seasonal than planting — fencing, decking, paving, drainage and privacy screens all hold up through winter, and three of those are already on your list. That is the winter offer, and it is a much better use of November than a general campaign.

What Is Genuinely Good
⭐ Credit where it is due
  • ⭐⭐ Booked solid for six months on nothing but word of mouth, in your first year. That is the single most impressive fact in this document. It means the work is good and people recommend you — which is the hard part, and it cannot be bought.
  • ⭐ A £25,000 job inside your first year. That tells us there is real money in what you do, and it is the number everything else should be built around.
  • You have a Completed Jobs page and a Customer Reviews page. Most firms your age have neither.
  • A clear, wide service list — fencing, raised beds, grass, ramps, paving, decking, water features, sleeper borders, pergolas, planters, privacy screens.
  • A working contact form with consent wording on it.
  • A landline as well as a mobile — 0191 and 07922. ⭐ Most sole operators have only a mobile, and a local landline reads as more established.
  • ⭐ You have never advertised, which means nothing is wasted and nothing is broken. Everything from here is upside, and there are no bad habits to undo.
The Rest Of The Problems
🔴 1. No measurement of any kind

No Google Analytics, no Tag Manager, no advertising tag, no pixel. Nothing.

⛔ This is not optional if you are about to advertise. Spending money on traffic you cannot measure means that in three months you will not know whether it worked, which enquiries came from where, or what a customer cost you. It goes on before the first pound of spend, not after.

🔴 2. Neither of your phone numbers is clickable

Both numbers appear as text only. There is not a single tap-to-call link on the site.

⚠️ Most people will see your site on a phone. Right now they have to memorise or copy a number to ring you. For an advertising campaign this is close to fatal — a large share of the people who want to call you simply will not.

⭐ It is a few minutes of work and it is the highest-return fix on this page.

🔴 3. Nothing about insurance, guarantees or accreditation

We found no mention of public liability insurance, any guarantee or warranty, or any scheme such as Checkatrade, TrustATrader or TrustMark.

🔴 Set that against a £25,000 job. A homeowner handing over that sum to a company incorporated eleven months ago is taking a visible risk, and your website currently gives them nothing to hold on to. Referred customers do not need it because somebody vouched for you. A stranger from an advert needs it badly.

⭐ If you already carry insurance and offer any guarantee, putting both on the site is a ten minute job and it is worth more than the first month of advertising.

⚠️ 4. A Gmail address on a company that owns its own domain

You own keetonslandscapingandfencing.co.uk and take enquiries at keetonsgardens@gmail.com.

⚠️ Two problems, and the second is the real one: it reads as smaller than you are, and the word in it is “gardens”, which reinforces exactly the maintenance positioning that is costing you.

⚠️ 5. Your sitemap declares one page

Your navigation offers six — home, about, contact, legal notice, completed jobs and customer reviews. Your sitemap tells Google about one. Your completed jobs and your reviews are the two most persuasive things you own, and nothing is pointing search engines at either.

⚠️ 6. Six hundred and fifty-nine words, and no job is described

The homepage is short, and the Completed Jobs page exists but there is no written account of a single project — what the garden was like, what you built, roughly what it cost, how long it took.

⭐ You have a £25,000 job. Written up properly, with photographs, that one page would do more to win you another £25,000 job than any advert we could write.

What We Could Not Verify
Said plainly
  • Your typical job value. We know the biggest was £25,000. ⭐ We have no idea what the average is, and on your service list it could be anything from a few hundred pounds upward. This is the first question on Monday.
  • Whether you carry public liability insurance or offer any guarantee. Neither appears on the site.
  • Whether the thin books are seasonal. You have had one season, so there is no pattern to read yet.
  • Whether you work alone or have anybody with you. The site says “we” throughout but names nobody and shows no team.
  • How much of your work is construction — fencing, paving, decking — versus maintenance. The site presents them as one business and they are not.
  • The “over 15 years of experience” on your site. ⭐ We read that as your own experience in the trade rather than the company's age, which is normal and fine — we simply have not verified it.
What We Would Want To Ask On Monday
  • 1. What is an average job worth? ⭐ Not the biggest. The typical one. Everything depends on this.
  • 2. What is the smallest job you will take? And would you rather never do them again?
  • 3. Of this year's work, how much was construction and how much was maintenance?
  • 4. Was last winter quiet too? ⚠️ Even one data point helps, and it decides whether we spend now or build now and spend in February.
  • 5. Do you work alone? It sets the ceiling on what more work would even mean.
  • 6. Are you insured, and do you guarantee your work? If yes, it is missing from your site.
  • 7. Can you send us photographs of the £25,000 job? ⭐ Before and after. It is the most valuable marketing asset you own and it is not on your website.
Question one changes everything else. ⭐ If your average job is £6,000 we have a very good business to advertise. If it is £900, we would be telling you to fix the positioning first and sell you nothing on Monday.

The work is clearly good. The website is selling the wrong half of it.
