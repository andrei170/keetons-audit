Why This Is Urgent Rather Than Interesting
🔴 Advertising amplifies whatever you are already saying

An advertising campaign is not a way of finding new customers in general. It is a way of finding more of the customer your website already describes. It is very good at that, which is precisely the problem.

🔴 So if we advertised your site as it stands, here is what would happen, in order:

  • Enquiries would rise. You would be pleased.
  • They would be dominated by small work — a fence panel, a bit of turf, a tidy-up — because that is what “comprehensive garden maintenance” and “regardless of budget” invite.
  • Your diary would fill. It would look like success.
  • Your revenue would rise far less than your hours, because you would be doing many small jobs instead of few large ones.
  • ⛔ And when the big spring jobs came up, you would have no capacity to take them, because you would be booked with £300 work sold at advertising prices.

⭐⭐ That is the genuine risk, and we would rather lose a month of billing than walk you into it. Fixing the words first costs you almost nothing. Fixing them after a season of wrong customers costs you the season.

⚠️ None of these lines is badly written. They are warm and human, and somebody put thought into them. They are simply selling a different, smaller business than the one that did a £25,000 job.

⛔ And “regardless of budget” appearing twice is the most expensive phrase on your website. It is an open invitation to the customer you cannot afford to serve.

What We Would Change, And What We Would Leave Alone
⭐ Change
  • Lead with construction, not maintenance. Fencing, paving, decking, patios, pergolas, privacy screens — the things you build. They are already on your list, just below the maintenance language.
  • Remove both “regardless of budget” lines. ⭐ Single highest-value edit on the site.
  • Write up the £25,000 job. What the garden was, what you built, how long, roughly what it cost, photographs before and after. One page.
  • Say what a project typically starts at. ⚠️ Uncomfortable, and the most effective filter there is. It costs you the enquiries you did not want.
  • Move the email to your own domain and off the word “gardens”.
  • Put insurance and any guarantee on the site, if you hold them.
  • Make both phone numbers tappable.
⛔ Leave alone — it is working
  • The warmth. ⭐ Your tone is a genuine asset in a trade full of people who do not turn up. Do not let anybody sand it off into corporate language.
  • The accessibility work. If low-maintenance and accessible gardens are work you want and are good at, keep it — as its own page, for the customer who wants it. The problem is it being the headline, not it existing.
  • Your reviews and completed jobs pages. They need filling out, not replacing.
  • The service breadth. Eleven services is fine when they are all construction. It was only a problem when maintenance led.
  • ⚠️ And do not drop maintenance if it fills your winter. It may be exactly the right work in January. It is simply not what should be advertised in March.
The Objection We Expect From You
“If I put prices and narrow it down, I will turn work away. My books are already thin”

You will turn work away. That is the intention and it is a fair thing to be nervous about when the diary is empty.

⭐ But look at what you have already proved. You filled six months in your first year on word of mouth alone, with no advertising at all. That is not a business that struggles to be wanted. It is a business whose demand arrives through one channel that happens to be quiet right now.

⭐⭐ And consider who refers you. A customer whose £25,000 garden you built tells their neighbours. A customer whose fence panel you replaced for £200 mostly does not. The large jobs are what produced the referrals that filled your six months. Chasing small work does not just pay less — it quietly cuts off the channel that has been feeding you.

⛔ If after Monday's conversation it turns out your average job is small and that is the business you actually want, we will say that plainly and we will not sell you a campaign. We would rather tell you that than take a retainer for work that cannot pay for itself.

“Regardless of budget” appears twice on your homepage.

It is the most expensive sentence you own, and deleting it is free.
