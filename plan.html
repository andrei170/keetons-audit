Build Through The Quiet Months, Be Ready Before Everyone Else

The Shape Of The Plan, And Why It Is Not “Start Advertising”
You asked for work booked in as soon as possible. Here is the honest route to it

⚠️ If your quiet period is seasonal — and October through January is the quiet period for almost every landscaper — then the enquiries you want most do not exist yet. They begin when people start thinking about their gardens again, which is roughly February.

⭐⭐ That is not bad news. It is a gift of about fourteen weeks. Most of your competitors will do nothing until March, then start advertising at the same moment, into the same auction, with the same unprepared websites. Being ready in January instead of March is the cheapest advantage available to you.

⛔ And there is a winter business too, which is where the near-term work comes from — fencing, decking, paving and drainage do not stop for the weather, and storm damage creates fencing work in exactly the months when planting work dies.

Phase One — October and November: Fix What Is Free To Fix
  • 1. Measurement on the site. ⛔ Non-negotiable and first. Analytics, and a record of every form submission and phone tap. Until this exists, nothing after it can be judged.
  • 2. Make both phone numbers tappable. Minutes of work. ⭐ Highest return of anything on this list.
  • 3. Delete both “regardless of budget” lines and bring construction to the front of the homepage. Free, and it changes who enquires.
  • 4. Write up the £25,000 job. ⭐ Photographs, what you built, roughly what it cost, how long it took. One page, and it is the most persuasive asset you will own.
  • 5. Move the email to your own domain.
  • 6. Put insurance and any guarantee on the site — if you hold them.
  • 7. Fix the sitemap so Google is told about all six pages instead of one.
  • 8. Ask your last ten customers for a Google review. ⭐ Free, and you have just come off six months of work by people who liked you enough to recommend you. This is the single best use of a November evening.
⭐ Note that phase one costs almost nothing and involves no advertising spend at all. It is mostly writing, a few technical fixes and some phone calls. If you did only this and nothing else, you would be in a materially better position by December.
Phase Two — November and December: The Winter Offer
⭐ Where the near-term work actually is

Three of the services you already offer hold up through winter, and one of them has a seasonal driver working in your favour:

  • Fencing and fence repair. ⭐⭐ Autumn and winter storms break fences. That demand is real, it is urgent when it happens, and it arrives in exactly the months your planting work stops. This is your winter lane.
  • Decking, paving and patios. Buildable in cold weather, and a homeowner planning a spring garden often wants the hard landscaping done first.
  • Privacy screens, sleeper borders, raised beds. Construction, not planting. Weather tolerant.

⭐ So a modest, tightly targeted winter campaign is defensible — aimed at fencing and hard landscaping specifically, in your own patch, not at “landscaping” in general. ⚠️ Small budget, treated as a test to learn what an enquiry costs you, not as the main event.

⛔ What we would not do is run a general garden campaign in December. It is the most expensive way to discover that nobody is thinking about their lawn.

Phase Three — January: Be Live Before The Season Opens
  • 1. Service pages for the money work. One properly written page each for fencing, paving, decking and full garden transformation — so a search for any one of them has somewhere to land.
  • 2. Two or three more jobs written up with photographs, alongside the £25,000 one.
  • 3. Google Business Profile properly filled out — services, area, photographs, and the reviews gathered in November. ⭐ For a local trade this is often worth more than a website.
  • 4. Campaigns built, tested and paused, ready to switch on. ⭐ Live by the last week of January, because the first enquiries of the season go to whoever is already there.
  • 5. An enquiry process that does not depend on you remembering. Every enquiry recorded, acknowledged automatically, and followed up on a timer.
Phase Four — February to May: Spend Properly
The season, and the only part where real money goes out of the door
  • Full campaigns live from early February, aimed at construction work and the job sizes you actually want.
  • Weekly review of what an enquiry costs and what proportion become jobs — which is possible for the first time, because phase one put the measurement in.
  • Spend moves toward whatever produces the larger jobs and away from whatever produces small ones.
  • ⚠️ And a capacity conversation, honestly. If spring advertising works and you are one person, you will hit the same wall two of this week's other businesses are already against. Better to decide in February whether you are subcontracting, hiring or simply booking further out.
⛔ What Would Make Us Change This Plan On Monday
The plan above rests on assumptions, and three of them could be wrong
  • If last winter was busy — then this is not seasonal, something else has changed, and we should act now rather than in February. The whole timeline moves forward.
  • If your average job is small — a few hundred pounds rather than thousands — then ⛔ we would not recommend advertising at all until the positioning has changed and you have proved you can win larger work consistently.
  • If you are working alone with no intention of growing — then a full season campaign could book you solid by March and leave you turning away the good jobs in June. ⭐ In that case the right answer is fewer, better enquiries, not more of them, and the plan gets smaller and more selective.

⭐ We would rather show you a plan we are willing to tear up than one that sounds certain. Monday decides which version you get.

Everyone else starts advertising in March.

Fourteen quiet weeks is enough to be ready in January instead.
